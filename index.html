The Challenge

Girls lose interest in STEM before high school — and we can change that early.

Research consistently shows that gender disparities in STEM begin in childhood. By the time girls reach secondary school, many have already formed limiting beliefs about their abilities in science and technology.

The good news: early, hands-on intervention works. When girls ages 9–12 are positioned as inventors and engineers, their self-belief transforms. This is the window. Makey Makey opens it.

The Research

A 3-year peer-reviewed study. Real schools. Real results.

Researchers at Universitat Pompeu Fabra in Barcelona embedded Makey Makey into a Design Thinking project-based learning program across 12 public primary schools, measuring self-efficacy and motivation before and after — across 318 students over three years.

Key Findings — Girl Students

Girls didn't just improve. They outperformed every expectation.

Compared to boys, girls showed larger improvements across more measures. Here's what the numbers actually mean.

What is "effect size"?
A measure of how meaningful the change was — not just whether it happened. Researchers consider 0.30+ to be educationally significant. These results hit 0.41.
What does p < 0.001 mean?
Less than a 1-in-1,000 chance the result is a coincidence. The scientific gold standard is p < 0.05. These results far exceed it — we can be 99.9% confident.
What is the 1–5 scale?
Students rated agreement: 1 = Not at all, 3 = Neither yes nor no, 5 = Definitely yes. The before → after scores show real shifts in how girls see themselves.
