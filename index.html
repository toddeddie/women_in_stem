When Girls Invent,
They Believe They Can.

A landmark 3-year study found that hands-on making with Makey Makey produces significantly stronger gains in STEM confidence for girls — at exactly the age it matters most.

📄 Santos et al. (2025) · International Journal of Technology and Design Education · Springer Nature · DOI: 10.1007/s10798-025-10001-6 · Open Access
The Challenge

Girls lose interest in STEM before high school — and we can change that early.

Research consistently shows gender disparities in STEM begin in childhood. By the time girls reach secondary school, many have already formed limiting beliefs about science and technology.

Early, hands-on intervention works. When girls ages 9–12 are positioned as inventors, their self-belief transforms. This is the window. Makey Makey opens it.

The Research

A 3-year peer-reviewed study. Real schools. Real results.

Researchers embedded Makey Makey into a Design Thinking project-based learning program across 12 public primary schools in Barcelona, measuring self-efficacy and motivation before and after — across 318 students over three years.

The Program

Five sessions. Real invention. Real confidence.

Students work through a Design Thinking journey — empathizing with a real problem, ideating, and building an interactive prototype with Makey Makey and Scratch. No prior experience needed.

1
Empathize & Define
Explore a real-world problem and define what to solve
2
Ideate
Brainstorm solutions with sticky notes and group discussion
3
Prototype
Sketch and plan the invention's features and design
4
Build & Test
Use Makey Makey + Scratch to bring the prototype to life
5
Demo
Present inventions to peers and reflect on the process

Notably, girls showed marked improvements in self-efficacy following the intervention, suggesting that the design of these activities may help counteract early gender disparities in STEM. These findings reinforce the importance of implementing inclusive, learner-centered pedagogies at a stage when students are forming foundational beliefs about their abilities and interests in STEM.
