The Challenge

Girls lose interest in STEM before high school — and we can change that early.

Research consistently shows that gender disparities in STEM begin in childhood. By the time girls reach secondary school, many have already formed limiting beliefs about their abilities in science and technology.

The good news: early, hands-on intervention works. When girls ages 9–12 are positioned as inventors and engineers, their self-belief transforms. This is the window. Makey Makey opens it.

The Research

A 3-year peer-reviewed study. Real schools. Real results.

Researchers at Universitat Pompeu Fabra in Barcelona embedded Makey Makey into a Design Thinking project-based learning program across 12 public primary schools. They measured self-efficacy and motivation in Science, Technology, and Design — before and after — across 318 students over three years.

Girls didn't just improve. They outperformed every expectation.

The Program

Five sessions. Real invention. Real confidence.

Students engage in a structured Design Thinking journey — empathizing with a real-world problem, brainstorming solutions, and building an interactive prototype using Makey Makey and Scratch. No prior experience required.

1

Empathize & Define

Students explore a real-world problem — like ocean pollution — and define what they want to solve.

2

Ideate

Teams brainstorm solutions and build a "wall of ideas" with sticky notes and collaborative discussion.

3

Prototype

Students choose features for their invention and sketch their technological prototype design.

4

Build & Test

Using Makey Makey + Scratch, students bring their prototype to life as an interactive invention.

5

Demo

Teams present their inventions to peers, reflecting on what worked and what they'd improve next.
